# The Ancient Canon — Ancient Library Research Corpus

Status: **research/translation branch — not yet part of the primary 81-book canon UI**

## Editorial rule

The app must keep the primary 81-book biblical collection visually and conceptually distinct from extracanonical, pseudepigraphal, Apostolic Fathers, patristic, martyr, and canon-history material. Ancient does not automatically mean canonical, and traditional attribution does not automatically mean historical authorship.

Every work added to the Ancient Library should carry an **About This Text** record with: title and alternate titles; corpus/category; conventional and disputed date ranges; claimed/traditional author; historical authorship assessment; original/extant languages; principal manuscript/recension information; oldest material witness where known; canonical/reception history; relationship to biblical passages; source edition used for translation; translation confidence; and notes on significant variants, lacunae, interpolation, or redaction.

## Translation standard

1. Produce a **fresh modern-English translation** from the earliest responsibly accessible source-language text or a public-domain critical transcription. Older English translations may be used as comparison witnesses but should not control the wording.
2. Do not copy modern copyrighted translations into the app.
3. Preserve established chapter/section numbering so users can compare editions.
4. Mark supplied/restored text, lacunae, damaged text, uncertain words, conjectures, and recension differences instead of hiding them.
5. Do not harmonize a text to the Bible or to later doctrine. Translate what the witness says and explain differences in notes.
6. When several recensions materially differ, either provide separate views or a clearly documented base text with variant notes.
7. Fragments must identify the later author/manuscript that preserves the quotation.
8. Dates are ranges, not false precision. Where scholarship is divided, the app should say so.

# A. Full corpus from Rutherford H. Platt Jr., *The Forgotten Books of Eden* (1926)

The Sacred Texts link supplied for this project contains the following works. Platt is useful as a public-domain historical English witness and as a checklist, but the 1926 popular edition is **not** our scholarly base translation.

## Adam literature

### First Book of Adam and Eve
- Work behind the popular title: *Conflict of Adam and Eve with Satan*, Book I in the Malan/Platt division.
- Type: Christian Adam literature / extracanonical.
- Date: current literary complex is generally treated as late antique; a fifth–sixth-century setting is often proposed, but the development and transmission require explicit qualification.
- Textual tradition: extant Geʿez; strong evidence of an Arabic antecedent; relationship to Syriac Adam/Cave of Treasures traditions must be documented rather than assumed.
- Translation note: do **not** label this a lost chapter of Genesis or confuse it with the much earlier *Life of Adam and Eve / Apocalypse of Moses* tradition.

### Second Book of Adam and Eve
- Same textual complex, continuing the patriarchal/antediluvian narrative.
- Type: Christian Adam literature / extracanonical.
- Translation note: preserve its own claims but clearly label its later Christian setting and transmission.

## Enochic literature

### 2 Enoch / Slavonic Enoch / Book of the Secrets of Enoch
- Type: Enochic pseudepigrapha.
- Date: highly disputed. A first-century CE Jewish core is widely argued, but later proposals and substantial transmission/redaction questions remain.
- Principal extant language: Church Slavonic, in differing recensions; a lost Greek and/or Semitic antecedent has been proposed.
- Translation rule: do not silently merge the long and short recensions.

## Second Temple Jewish and early Christian literature

### Psalms of Solomon
- 18 psalms.
- Usually located in the mid-first century BCE in the aftermath of Pompey’s conquest of Jerusalem in 63 BCE.
- Survives in Greek and Syriac; a Hebrew original is widely argued.

### Odes of Solomon
- 41 of an original 42 odes survive.
- Early Christian hymnody, commonly dated from the late first into the second century CE; exact date and original language remain debated.
- Principal witness is Syriac, with Greek/Coptic evidence.
- Must not be confused with the Psalms of Solomon.

### Letter of Aristeas
- Hellenistic Jewish work in Greek.
- Most current scholarship places it in the second century BCE, though proposals extend wider.
- Anonymous Alexandrian Jewish author writing as “Aristeas.”
- Essential for the history of the legend of the Greek Pentateuch/Septuagint; literary claims and historical reconstruction must be distinguished.

### 4 Maccabees
- Greek Jewish philosophical/martyr discourse.
- Common proposed dates fall roughly between 19 and 135 CE; exact date remains debated.
- Reception/canonical status varies by Christian tradition and should be displayed rather than flattened into “canonical/noncanonical.”

### Story and Wisdom of Ahiqar (Ahikar)
- Ancient Near Eastern wisdom narrative.
- A late-fifth-century BCE Aramaic copy from Elephantine is the oldest known exemplar of this literary tradition; later Syriac, Arabic, Armenian and other recensions expand/adapt it.
- Ancient Aramaic core and later narrative forms should be distinguished in the app.

## Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs

Translate and index each testament separately:
- Testament of Reuben
- Testament of Simeon
- Testament of Levi
- Testament of Judah
- Testament of Issachar
- Testament of Zebulun
- Testament of Dan
- Testament of Naphtali
- Testament of Gad

Text-critical warning: the complete surviving corpus is transmitted through Christian manuscripts and contains distinctly Christian material. A substantial body of scholarship argues that much underlying material is Jewish/Hasmonean with later Christian interpolation, while other scholars give more weight to Christian composition/redaction. The app must **show this dispute** and flag important recension/interpolation questions instead of deleting suspected Christian material without notice.

# B. Apostolic Fathers — Core Ancient Church Library

A modern Apostolic Fathers corpus normally includes 1 Clement, 2 Clement, Ignatius, Polycarp, Didache, Barnabas, Papias, Hermas, Martyrdom of Polycarp, Diognetus and Quadratus. These should be the first early-Christian layer added after the linked Platt corpus.

## Clement
- **1 Clement** — anonymous Roman church letter traditionally associated with Clement of Rome; c. 96 CE is a common working date, with normal scholarly qualifications.
- **2 Clement** — anonymous early Christian homily, not actually a second letter written by Clement; generally second century and often placed mid-to-late second century.

## Didache
- **Didache / Teaching of the Twelve Apostles** — church order/catechesis. Scholars commonly date the work or its layers between the latter half of the first century and first half of the second; composite/redactional development is likely.

## Ignatius of Antioch — translate the seven-letter middle recension first
- To the Ephesians
- To the Magnesians
- To the Trallians
- To the Romans
- To the Philadelphians
- To the Smyrnaeans
- To Polycarp

Editorial warning: the **middle recension of seven letters** is the form used by most modern editions, but authenticity and exact dating remain debated. The later long recension and other spurious/pseudonymous Ignatian letters should eventually be available in a separate “Later Ignatian Tradition” section, never mixed into the seven-letter corpus without a label.

## Polycarp and Smyrna
- **Polycarp to the Philippians** — depending on theories of unity/composition, commonly dated roughly c. 110–140 CE.
- **Martyrdom of Polycarp** — relates the martyrdom usually dated 155/156 CE; the date and development of the final literary form require notes.

## Other core Apostolic Fathers
- **Epistle of Barnabas** — anonymous, usually late first/early second century; not securely written by the New Testament Barnabas.
- **Shepherd of Hermas** — Roman Christian apocalypse/paraenesis, first half of the second century; c. 140 remains a common working point, with compositional questions.
- **Fragments of Papias** — Papias flourished in the early second century; his five-book work is lost, so every fragment must identify the later source preserving it (especially Irenaeus/Eusebius and later witnesses).
- **Epistle to Diognetus** — anonymous apology; date/provenance/recipient are uncertain and many scholars favor a later second-century date.
- **Fragment of Quadratus** — early apology fragment, preserved by Eusebius; traditional association with Hadrian’s reign.

# D. Canon-history and documentary witnesses

These texts are exceptionally useful to a Bible app because they let the user see *how early Christians talked about Scripture and books*.

- **Muratorian Fragment** — show both the traditional late-second-century dating and serious later-date arguments.
- **Eusebius, Ecclesiastical History — source-critical selections** — especially passages preserving Papias, Quadratus, Hegesippus, Dionysius and lists/discussions of disputed books. The app must distinguish Eusebius’s own words from quotations he preserves.
- **Athanasius, Festal Letter 39 (367 CE)** — later than the Ante-Nicene corpus but essential for canon history because of its New Testament list and distinction among categories of books.
- Later canon lists can be a separate timeline, not mixed into “Apostolic Fathers.”

# F. Initial scholarly/source-control bibliography

Research should be checked against modern scholarship while translations themselves are built from legally usable source texts.

- Michael F. Bird and Scott Harrower, eds., *The Cambridge Companion to the Apostolic Fathers* (Cambridge University Press, 2021).
- Clayton N. Jefford / William Varner rev., “Apostolic Fathers,” *Oxford Bibliographies* (updated 2023).
- Modern Cambridge/Oxford/SBL scholarship for individual dates, provenance and reception.
- Public-domain J. B. Lightfoot / Apostolic Fathers editions as historical source-text/translation comparison where appropriate.
- Public-domain *Ante-Nicene Fathers* series as a comparison witness and discovery index, not as unquestioned modern scholarship.
- Rutherford H. Platt Jr., ed., *The Forgotten Books of Eden* (1926) as the user-supplied collection/checklist, not as the controlling translation.
- S. C. Malan, *The Book of Adam and Eve, Also Called the Conflict of Adam and Eve with Satan* (1882), for the historical English/Geʿez-transmission tradition; reassess against source editions.
- Current specialist work on 2 Enoch, Psalms of Solomon, Odes of Solomon, Aristeas, 4 Maccabees, Ahiqar, and Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs before locking metadata.

## Research references already checked for this branch
- Oxford Classical Dictionary / Oxford Research Encyclopedia treatment of the *Letter of Aristeas*: modern scholarship broadly favors a second-century BCE date and identifies the work as an Alexandrian Jewish composition.
- SBL scholarship on the *Psalms of Solomon*: corpus reflects the Roman conquest of Jerusalem in 63 BCE; specialists widely argue for a Hebrew original behind Greek/Syriac transmission.
- Encyclopedia of Ancient History treatment of Ahiqar: the Elephantine papyrus is a late-fifth-century BCE copy and represents the oldest known Aramaic literary work in that description.
- Cambridge/Oxford work on the Apostolic Fathers: the collection is a modern scholarly grouping and spans roughly the end of the first through the second century, with individual dates disputed.
- Recent Ignatian scholarship: the seven-letter middle recension remains the standard form used by modern editions, while authenticity and exact dating continue to be debated.

# G. Translation build order

**Pass 1 — metadata before prose:** establish source edition, witnesses, recensions, section numbering, and dating/authorship notes for every work.

**Pass 2 — linked corpus:** First/Second Adam and Eve; 2 Enoch; Psalms of Solomon; Odes of Solomon; Aristeas; 4 Maccabees; Ahiqar; Twelve Patriarchs.

**Pass 3 — Apostolic Fathers:** 1 Clement → Didache → seven Ignatian letters → Polycarp → Martyrdom of Polycarp → Barnabas → Hermas → Papias → 2 Clement → Diognetus → Quadratus.

**Pass 4 — major Ante-Nicene writers:** Justin → Irenaeus → Melito/Apologists → Clement of Alexandria → Tertullian → Perpetua → Hippolytus → Origen → Cyprian and third-century corpus.

**Pass 5 — app integration:** full-text search, read-aloud, timeline filters, canonical-status filters, manuscript/source panels, verse-to-ancient-text cross-links, and source/variant notes.

No work should be marked “translated” until its source text and recension are recorded.
